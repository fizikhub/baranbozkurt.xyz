# UX Heuristics & Cognitive Laws

## Overview
Heuristic evaluation identifies design flaws by evaluating interfaces against established human-computer interaction (HCI) psychological principles.

## Core Laws of UX
1. **Fitts's Law**: The time to acquire a target is a function of the distance to and size of the target. (Make critical buttons larger and closer to the user's thumb/cursor).
2. **Hick's Law**: The time it takes to make a decision increases logarithmically with the number and complexity of choices. (Limit options; use progressive disclosure).
3. **Jakob's Law**: Users spend most of their time on other sites. They expect your site to work the same way as all the other sites they already know. (Stick to standard UI conventions).
4. **Miller's Law**: The average person can only keep 7 (plus or minus 2) items in their working memory. (Chunk complex data into discrete digestible sections).

## Nielsen Norman 10 Heuristics Checklist
1. Visibility of system status.
2. Match between system and the real world.
3. User control and freedom (undo/redo).
4. Consistency and standards.
5. Error prevention (confirmations for destructive actions).
6. Recognition rather than recall.
7. Flexibility and efficiency of use.
8. Aesthetic and minimalist design.
9. Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors.
10. Help and documentation.
